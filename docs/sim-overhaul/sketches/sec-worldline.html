Worldline the canvas tilts into spacetime

The bubble chamber is gone. It was a detector's picture of particles, and Worldline's bodies, bosons and fields don't behave like tracks in a liquid. The spacetime view moves from the side panel onto the main canvas instead. The canvas opens as the plane you know. Drag down and it tilts, and every trail rises into the world line it was the shadow of: orbits become helices, light becomes 45° lines, a pion's short life becomes a slanted stub ending in two photons.

Worldline already records every world line for signal delay and the Frames tab, so the view only reads what the sim keeps. The boost slider and the Lab and Observer buttons show one spacetime two ways. In lab coordinates the observer's present is a tilted plane. In the observer's own coordinates the plane lies flat and everything else shears, while light stays on its 45° lines.

What stays from the plate

Proper-time beads, photon color by Doppler shift, combed field textures for E and B, contour relief for the scalar fields, and a real star field behind the lensing plane. All of them read the model; none of them needed the bubble chamber.

Where the side plot goes

The Frame tab's spacetime diagram becomes redundant once the canvas itself tilts. The tab keeps the frame controls and the lag readout and drops the plot.

Build

A second camera in the existing WebGPU pipeline: instanced line strips from the history buffer, an orbit camera that eases between the plane and an oblique view, and the boost applied as a Lorentz transform in the vertex shader. The 2D view is the same camera looking straight down the time axis, so the two never disagree.
