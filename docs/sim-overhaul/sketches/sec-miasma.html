Miasma the atlas

The hex lattice becomes a plate from a nineteenth-century epidemiological atlas, in the line of John Snow's 1854 cholera map. The disease is the red; immunity is stipple; vaccination is hatching; deaths are small crosses. Contour lines record the day the wave arrived, so the plate keeps the outbreak's history after the wave has passed. You can see where it raced, where it stalled, and where a vaccinated block bent it.

The 3D surface from the first draft is gone. Miasma's world is a hexagon, and its six topologies come from gluing its three pairs of opposite edges. The plate now shows that the way a topology textbook draws a fundamental polygon. Chevrons mark the glued edges, one, two or three per pair, and their direction says whether a pair is glued straight, with a twist, or through the center. Faded copies of the world sit beyond each glued edge, so a wave visibly leaves one edge and carries on from its partner. Edges that are not glued are shores, drawn with a heavier rule and hachures.

Around the plate

The stacked-area counts become Nightingale's rose of 1858, the original epidemic graphic. The lineage forest becomes a time-scaled phylogeny. The oncoviral preset reskins the same lattice as an H&E-stained histology slide, so the two readings of Z become two specimens.

A question about Klein

The header comment in topology.js says Klein glues all three edge pairs, like the torus. wrapKlein glues pair 1 with a twist and pair 2 straight, and leaves pair 3 open, so the sketch draws pair 3 as a shore. Which one is intended? With pair 3 open, the surface still has a boundary.

Build

A fragment shader draws the lattice from a state texture (WebGPU, with a Canvas fallback). The faded copies are the same texture drawn under three transforms, and contours run on the CPU every few ticks. Medium.
