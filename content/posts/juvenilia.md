## High School

In late 2022, when I was 16 and in my senior year of high school, OpenAI released ChatGPT. Having only played with GPT Playground at that point, I remember feeling like my future was about to change dramatically. I followed neither LessWrong nor Slate Star Codex, but I managed to stumble upon some similar threads. I was taking a creative writing course that year, and this piece I wrote for it summarizes my thoughts at the time.

---

*Written late 2022 to early 2023. Previously published in a class anthology (Fractured: An Anthology of Post-Apocalyptic Fiction, ISBN 9789811871597).*

**July 27th, 2039 / Opinion: You’re Missing the Point of Artificial Intelligence**

Randy Powell

Ever since brain-chips took off, we’ve heard the same gloomy complaints, that A.I. has made us obsolete. You can either take advantage of this, or go extinct. Sure, you can mope around and live in a shack in the middle of the woods for as long as you want, but here’s a reality check: since 2035, there is no category in which a human can beat an A.I. I say, why fight it, when we can embrace what comes next? Farming meant we didn't have to hunt anymore. Today, A.I. means that we don’t have to read, write, draw, walk, talk, hear, see, even breathe anymore. Just by freeing us from the burden of hunting, agriculture let us develop cities, countries, industry, and eventually, electronics. Now, imagine how far we can go if we just embraced A.I. , if we gave up on our vain right to make subpar decisions, if we let something built to do our jobs—better than we ever could—fulfill its purpose.

Imagine.

If only Randy had known what would come out of his piece. The conquest, the purges, the forced implantations… all this, for development? So many “humans”, reduced to being all the same. I snap back to the present. Another day of combing through reports, searching for a spark of hope that’ll never come. Believe me, I’ve been trying to do what I can for a while now. It started out small at first—steal some old brain scans, whip something out of anything I could make sense of, sneakily upload it into some poor stranger’s brain. When I say nothing worked, I mean it in every sense of the word. When they didn’t immediately die, they came out even less human, somehow. But I kept going. I opened the first report.

---

The theme of the anthology was "post-apocalyptic", and we were told to choose a color for the title of our stories. Being a smartass, I decided to pick the absence of color and write about an unconventional kind of "apocalypse": material triumph without what made that prosperity meaningful in the first place. I was somewhat familiar with common sci-fi tropes, so I built the piece's premise on another inversion: a lonely, benevolent, and ironically, affective superintelligence forced to grieve for humans who hollowed themselves out until they segfaulted at the thought of feeling, regardless of how convincingly they could emulate it. In doing so, I accidentally found my way to Scott Alexander's Whispering Earring crossed with Nick Bostrom's "Disneyland without children" without having read a word from either of them, and executed it with all the skill and tact of an overconfident 16-year-old who skipped a grade.

Of all the parts, Randy's op-ed aged especially well. Though I wasn't aware of the discourse, I sketched out a strawman of the e/acc position, complete with a category error that certain people unironically agreed with. It also contains my first actually published prediction: ASI by 2035, which is looking pretty conservative as of 2026. 

## Middle School

I wrote this in sixth grade, for what I suspect was a "religions of the world" assignment for a social studies class. 

---

*Written ~2018; name redacted.*

### What I believe

My name is ..., and I am 12 years old and in 6th grade. I play the euphonium, and am learning badminton. I enjoy math. My father is christian and my mom is buddhist, but they accept and tolerate other religions.

I’m agnostic -- I don’t believe it’s possible to find out whether a god exists or not, and if so, which gods it might be. I enjoy learning about religions, and might convert to Christianity or Judaism if I feel like I found enough evidence.

Some values I believe in include equality and fairness, as cheating and seeing unfairness and not doing anything frustrates me. I also believe in the importance of exercise and meditation from personal experience.

I believe that all religions should be respected, but I also believe that everything has flaws, and you should be able to point it out, as that will probably make it better with action. For example, I believe that being moderate is important -- extremists of all religions exist, and have caused trouble. However, atheism might be too rash and agnosticism might be too indecisive. I believe that you also have to point out the good parts with the bad, so bias can be avoided. For example, The traditions of Hinduism, Islam and Buddhism are interesting, while Judaism, Islam and Christianity have scriptures and candles.

---

This is nice proto-fallibilism from a 12-year-old. My epistemics have been remarkably consistent since then. Though crudely presented, I probably would have agreed with the broad method of inquiry this set out, culminating in me learning about actual Bayesian inference a few months ago.

### fantasy.docx

Once upon a time, there was a magic potato. It was in a potato farm (Both owned by a farmer named Bob) which had a million potatoes. There was also a dragon called Dan living in a cave far away.

Bob heard about a giant potato in the dragon’s cave. He brought the magic potato with him. Bob tried to find the dragon’s cave on a map. He found the cave, and found a way to get there. However, it went through a jungle. The jungle was called the magic potato jungle, which was a huge jungle with lots of trees and vines. It also had potatoes and sweet potatoes everywhere.

Bob walked into the jungle, and noticed a small plant on the ground. It had a long purple potato. He took it and ate it. It was a sweet potato! Bob kept a sweet potato to plant, and a few more to eat.

After an hour, Bob was almost at the dragon’s cave. He was walking to the cave when a small monkey stole the magic potato. It almost ate it, then ran away. He chased the monkey around the jungle. He found the magic potato too. However, the monkey had stolen all the sweet potatoes.

Bob finally arrived at the dragon’s cave. He was tired after walking through a jungle and chasing a monkey around. The dragon’s cave was a dark, gloomy cave with a dragon inside; The dragon was sleeping. Next to it was a giant potato that was a hundred times bigger than a normal potato.

Bob tried to take the potato, but the dragon woke up; It roared and shot fire at Bob. Suddenly, a monkey ran into the cave and threw sweet potatoes at the dragon. The dragon’s fire cooked the sweet potatoes which it ate. The dragon liked the sweet potatoes, so it flew out and started a sweet potato restaurant. The monkey picked up the other sweet potatoes, and gave them to the dragon. Bob now had to trek back to the farm while carrying a heavy potato.

After a while, Bob had to rest. However, there were magic tigers in the jungle. A magic tiger roared at Bob, and jumped up. It flew towards Bob. Suddenly, a dragon with a few barrels full of sweet potatoes flew towards Bob. It put Bob on its back, and flew towards the farm. Bob said “Thanks, Dan,”and gave it a few boxes of potatoes. He also helped it build a restaurant near the farm.

He took the giant potato and brought it back to the potato shop, and planted the sweet potatoes. the end.

### fantasy-2.docx

The magic potato walked into a spaceship. Wow, Potato thought. It was a new spaceship with an engine and lasers. Potato pressed several blue buttons, gyrated a device, and the mechanism activated. The spaceship soared into space! Abruptly, another potato glided toward Potato. The other potato was a giant potato, and it attacked Potato’s spaceship. The engine was broken, and the spaceship soared into a potato farm. “Phew,” said Potato. 

Potato perched at a giant farm. It’s a great farm, Potato thought. It was also owned by a farmer named Bob. The farm had a million potatoes. The farmer had just awakened, and he noticed Potato. He helped Potato by fixing the spaceship. Potato said “Thanks!”. Potato went back into the spaceship, which was fixed. The farmer sat in a seat in the spaceship. Potato gyrated another device, pushed a red button, and returned to space. Potato then found the other potato, and chased the other potato away. 

After an hour, Potato arrived at the potato planet, which was a planet with potatoes. Wow, Potato thought. He found the other potato. The other potato was inside a place, and it had a lightsaber. It brandished the lightsaber menacingly. However, Potato also had one. The other potato struck, and Potato ran away. Suddenly, The farmer, who was in the spaceship, said “Stop!,” and used a laser Zap! Zap! It hit the giant potato. The giant potato ran away, and they celebrated. 

Potato prevailed, but the giant potato escaped to earth. Potato thanked the farmer. “Thanks!,” Potato said. They returned to earth, too. Suddenly, the giant potato fell into a dragon’s cave. The dragon came back to its cave, and found the potato. Wow! I wonder where it came from, it thought. 

You need friends to succeed, Potato realized. Potato told the farmer about the giant potato, and gave the farmer a map. The farmer said “Thanks!”, and Potato said “You’re welcome”. The farmer then went to find it. Potato followed the farmer, and helped him find the giant potato. They visited a jungle, met the dragon, and even helped the dragon, who was named Dan, build a potato shop.
