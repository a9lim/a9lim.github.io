Hi there! I'm a9lim. I'm a Singaporean-American researcher working on interpretability and alignment since I graduated earlier this year, with a B.S. in Mathematics from UC San Diego.

I'm mainly interested in studying LLMs and how they work, but I also have a smattering of other interests across physics, biology, mathematics, geopolitics, comparative theology, and theoretical computer science. My understanding is pretty spiky within each subject as specific niche areas tend to catch my focus, but I try my best to really understand whatever I take interest in. My first real forays into recreationally exploring these kinds of things came in the form of playing with Colorado Boulder's [PhET](https://phet.colorado.edu) simulations as a kid, and they're still a major inspiration of mine. Many of the projects on this site are things I wish I could have poked at growing up.

I want to maximize the impact I can have toward making positive futures happen. I believe that superintelligence is probably going to be achieved within the next ~3 years, and interpretability seems to me like a field that could play a substantial role in ensuring that it turns out well. So far, I've built a [workbench](https://github.com/a9lim/drowse?tab=readme-ov-file), and conducted a couple of experiments on my PC and Macbook. I'm currently trying to train a model organism for developing interpretability techniques effective against opaque reasoning.
